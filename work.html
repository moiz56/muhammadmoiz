What I do and What I Learnt

I work on industrial defect detection using computer vision. Customers send us images of their components containing defects, and my work runs from that raw data to a deployed model and the conversation with the customer about what it can and cannot do.

  • I coordinate and lead the annotation process, resolving inconsistencies between annotators and defining the defect scope and edge cases before labelling starts. What counts as a defect, and how borderline cases are treated, decides what the model can learn at all.
  • After annotation we split the data into training and test sets while preventing data leakage. If the same physical component is photographed from several angles, those images cannot be split across train and test, because the model would be evaluated on parts it had already seen. Splitting at the component level rather than the image level changed how I read evaluation results.
  • I have worked on two kinds of teams. The first runs proof-of-concept projects, where we determine whether a computer vision solution is viable for a customer. Through this I learned how to evaluate different approaches, how to choose and interpret the right metrics, and how to present technical results professionally to customers.
  • The second kind handles follow-up projects, where models are retrained and improved as new data arrives. I developed a retraining pipeline and learned to benchmark model iterations against a curated golden set, so versions are compared consistently instead of against whatever test split happened to exist.
  • I learned to document results properly: what changed between runs, which configuration produced which numbers, and why a version was accepted or rejected, so a result can still be traced weeks later.

Tech Stack and Techniques Used

  • The main techniques I have worked with are image tiling, synthetic defect generation, oversampling, and hyperparameter tuning.
  • I mostly fine-tune YOLO and DETR models for both object detection and segmentation.
  • The projects have covered PCB boards, industrial rings, microscopic imagery, dental imagery, pens, and Cardiobridge, which span very different scales, lighting conditions, and definitions of what a defect looks like.
  • I also contributed to AIDA, an internal Slack-based development agent, working on LLM-powered tool-calling workflows intended to reduce development time.
  • I work remotely with data scientists and engineers from multiple countries, take part in weekly technical discussions about improving our approaches, and develop on remote environments and VMs over SSH.
