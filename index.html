I am a computer science researcher and data scientist who has worked at the intersection of deep learning, computer vision, and healthcare. I completed my graduate research at the National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST), where I focused on medical image analysis and AI-driven approaches for cardiac magnetic resonance imaging.

My work in medical imaging has given me a strong foundation in computer vision, deep learning, and applied AI, from developing and evaluating vision models to working with the practical constraints of clinical data.

My coursework and independent work in language models have complemented my research experience in vision, giving me a foundation across both modalities. I am particularly interested in growing toward multimodal AI and exploring how vision and language models can be combined to build systems that reason across different forms of information, particularly in healthcare and scientific applications.

At the Deep Learning Lab at NUST, I worked on cardiac MRI under the supervision of Dr. Faisal Shafait and Dr. Momina Moetesum, in collaboration with cardiovascular MRI clinicians. My research addressed practical problems in cardiac MRI, including segmentation, image quality, and automated frequency offset selection. Our project won Best Adjudged Industrial Project at SEECS Open House 2026, where we presented and defended our research to a panel of external evaluators.

I currently work as a Data Scientist at Elunic AG, where I develop computer vision systems for automated defect detection in industrial components. My work spans the complete machine learning pipeline, from annotation and dataset design to model development, evaluation, and deployment.

Research

My research has focused on medical image analysis, particularly segmentation and prediction tasks involving cardiac MRI. A recurring theme in my work has been the challenge of developing reliable models when clinical datasets are small and image quality can vary substantially.

I am particularly interested in the practical considerations behind deploying computer vision models: how architectural choices interact with acquisition settings, annotation costs, computational constraints, and the requirements of real-world clinical systems.

Selected Work

Comparing 2D and 3D CNN Architectures for Left Ventricle Segmentation in Cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging

Muhammad Moiz, Abdul Rehman, Abdul Rehman, Muhammad Usman, Momina Moetesum, Faisal Shafait.

In revision, targeting ISBI 2027.

Evaluated five segmentation architectures in both 2D and 3D settings on the EMIDEC dataset, comparing segmentation accuracy, boundary error, volume difference, and computational cost. SegResNet2D achieved a Dice score of 0.929 and an HD95 of 0.67 mm.
